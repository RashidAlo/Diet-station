Layout laws

Two decrees from the home rounds (2026-09-09) that govern how the spacing scale gets applied — written here beside the inset system they constrain — and a third, about rows that mix type sizes.

1 · The uniform-padding law

Cards wear one padding value on all four sides — nothing may sit closer to one edge than another. The subscription plan cards (DS 1.0 component 4539-10208) set the reference: padding 20 everywhere, radius 32.

The DS tab bar at three plain links renders compact 256, centered. 3 · The mixed-size row

Any row that sets one large figure beside smaller satellites aligns their TOPS, and align-items: flex-start alone will not do it. Each run’s cap sits a fixed fraction of its own font-size below its box top, so the big one starts lower and the small ones ride high. The satellite needs margin-top: calc(bigSize × kBig − 1em × kSmall), where k is that fraction. Generalised here at the Calendar & Meals lane’s prompting: this began as a price rule, but it belongs to the shape, not the subject — a macro readout (480 beside Kcal), a duration beside its unit, a count beside its label. The worked implementation, the constants and the measuring recipe are on Price.

Measure k on a flat capital, and use that one value for every run in the row. Not on each run’s own glyphs — that is the tempting version and it is wrong. Measured on Urbane Rounded at the Calendar & Meals macro row’s five labels: P .184, F .184, C .1771 (round letters overshoot flat ones), Kcal .1493 (the l is an ascender), and g .3299 — no capital at all, so its ink starts at x-height. Per-run k spreads those across 0.18em; at their 6.45px that is 1.2px of visible disagreement, and it would shove the g nearly a pixel upward to stand its x-height on the figure’s cap line.
One cap-derived k puts every run on a common cap line and lets the rest fall where type says they should: ascenders overshoot, x-height letters sit lower, descenders hang. That is what running text does, and it is what the eye reads as aligned. Use a flat capital — H or P — since round letters and lining figures both overshoot by about .007em.

Elevation

Shadows are soft, warm-neutral and always double-layered: a hairline contact shadow plus a large, heavily-offset ambient blur with negative spread. Nothing floats hard.

Colored glows are allowed only on live/status controls, tinted with the control's own color — e.g. 0 5px 14px -6px rgba(52,199,89,.55) under a green publish button.
