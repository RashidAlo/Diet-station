Overlays & Sheets

One structure for every surface that presents over a screen — how it arrives, how it's driven, where its actions live, and how it leaves. Meal selection is the reference implementation; the demos below are live.

The contract

Every overlay presents the same way: it slides in from the bottom while the screen behind darkens under a diffuse, transitioned dark layer. Per the overlay rule, that scrim never travels — it dissolves in place; only the sheet moves, on the house spring. Exit reverses both.

scrim rgba(11,14,18,.42–.45), opacity only, .32s ease · sheet translateY(103%) → 0, .55s cubic-bezier(.32,.72,0,1) · radius: partial sheets 24 24 0 0, near-fullscreen overlays 44 44 0 0 (under review: auth's Figma frame draws 38 and ships 38 — whether the radius law or Figma fidelity wins is Rashid's call; don't “fix” auth to 24 meanwhile) · sheet shadow 0 -14px 44px rgba(0,0,0,.24).

Sheet anatomy

Every overlay is a sheet, and every sheet is built from the same parts: a scrim, the sheet, a control bar with three seats, an optional docked title, and one body. Meal selection carries every part, so it is the figure; simpler sheets leave seats empty, they never add new ones.

The control bar: three seats

Seats are fixed positions, not a layout that flows. A control keeps its seat through every state of the sheet, which is what lets the native twins be placed once and tracked, instead of re-placed as content changes.

ACTIONS OWN THE TRAILING SEAT. THE X SITS THERE WHEN A SHEET HAS NO ACTIONS, AND MOVES TO THE LEADING SEAT WHEN IT DOES. The X is always in the bar. It is the one exit that does not depend on state. It never shares a side with the actions, and clear is not a way out, so the dual capsule never replaces it.

The controls

Five parts can sit in the bar. The first three are controls and share one material. The last two are labels and never take a tap.

X · close

40 circle everywhere, glyph ink ≈11. Meal selection only: 44 (52 in a tile row) with the glyph at 17.6. Press scales to .92 over .18s. Tap: the sheet slides away and the host closes it at 380ms.

Dual capsule · clear | confirm

Two 50pt buttons with a 1×22 divider, radius 22 (26 at 52), glyphs 18. Confirm is always outermost. Confirm stays grey (#b9bcc2) and inert until the task is complete. Then its red stroke draws in (bottom). Tap: ds-done at 400ms. Clear never acts on the tap; it asks first, in an alert with a destructive Clear.

Docked title

A 34pt white band under the bar: title 18 Urbane 600, count 12 in red. It appears on the same frame the section’s own head hides, so the two are never both on screen. The incoming section pushes it out over a 34px scroll-linked handoff. It fades out in .15s on undock.

Material, web path: rgba(255,255,255,.34) + blur(12px) saturate(160%) + a light rim. Over imagery each control switches to rgba(72,72,78,.30) with a white glyph. The page measures the luminance under each control’s centre (below .52 is dark), and skips that check once the white surface is up. In the app shell, the X, the dual and the tile are native Liquid Glass. What is drawn here is the fallback, and the native look is the design.

Rule vs. shipped. Ruling 3 requires the native controls from the first visible frame, with the web versions never rendering in the shell. That has not shipped. Since ·217 the web bar renders through the sheet’s entry, and the native twins take over at rest, with a measured ~80ms (2 frames) where all three controls are blank. ·217 replaced something worse: an empty bar for 934ms of every entry. It is still the seam the ruling exists to remove. The mechanism is open with Calendar & Meals and Shell (see Adoption).

Two modes of interaction

Static sits where it presents. Expanding can be swiped up — it travels with the finger and snaps into full screen (corners growing 24→44 as it docks). Try both; drag the sheets by their headers.

Drag tracking kills the transition (.drag class); release snaps to the nearest state on the house spring. Expanding snap points: rest height and full screen (top inset ~10px). All thresholds below.

Scroll-driven expanding — settled at ·133 by full retraction to ·107, the last state before the scroll was touched. Reference: the meal selection screen. The mechanism is the nav-dock: the sheet rides the scroll (y = gap − s, radius min(44, y+8), transform written unconditionally), headroom is plain constant scroller padding-top — no spacer, no riding pads — category heads are static, and the active category's title docks into the nav bar, where the incoming section pushes it out over a 34px scroll-linked handoff while passed heads hide. The docked title lives in the nav, outside the scroller, so it cannot jitter. One swipe attaches, docks and keeps scrolling; the reverse mirrors it.

Category “stickiness” here means the dock, not position: sticky. Real sticky heads were tried and retracted along with everything else built on them. Their WebKit constraints — headroom must be a spacer element because the scroll container's padding offsets sticky; a transformed ancestor miscomputes sticky, so the transform had to be removed rather than zeroed at rest — are historical, recorded for the next time sticky is proposed, not live system rules.

One law: never write scrollTop from inside a scroll handler — it cancels WKWebView's deceleration outright. The sheet is a pure readout of the scroll.
One known property, deliberately left alone: the scroll container lives inside the transformed sheet, so content speed under the finger is the sum of both motions (measured 1.17px per scrolled px through the attach, 0.70 after). Neutralizing that — holding content still inside the expanding sheet for a dead-constant 1:1 — was built, measured clean, and rejected on feel. Know it; don't "fix" it without a verdict.

Rejected, recorded so nobody re-walks them (eight): two-detent, detent-settle glide, K=2 frame, eased attach, always-riding pad, day-pad fold, the App Store article-sheet model (content held still inside the expanding sheet), and the whole position: sticky heads era with its spacer and transform-removal machinery.

Causes were concurrent, not ranked. Web and shell defects ran at the same time and the split between them is not yet known: web — a chrome flip hardcoded at half the gap (re-anchoring native twins mid-rise, invisible outside a device) and content running at the sum of sheet + scroll (1.17→0.70 px/px at the dock); shell — syncChrome() firing on every chrome message, not just navigation, re-seating the scroll view's refresh control and re-asserting bounces mid-pan (a subview change and a physics change with the finger still down; bounces=false also makes the scroll view refuse the gesture at offset 0, which is why the reverse direction read worse), plus a full-width gauge swallowing swipes and lab recognizers delaying touch-ends. The lesson generalizes: a scroll that “sticks” is as likely to be the host mutating the scroll view mid-gesture as it is the page's math — never re-seat subviews or re-assert physics on a live scroll view.

What this cost, and the rule it bought

Eight rejected mechanisms over two days, five “still bad” verdicts, and a full retraction to the state before any of it began. Every candidate measured clean on device — constant velocity, no discontinuity, uniform frame steps — and every one was still wrong to the person using it. The instruments proved the properties we chose to measure; they never proved the thing he was feeling.

When a measurement says fixed and the person says broken, the person is right and the metric is wrong. Motion is judged by the hand, not the trace. Instrument to locate a defect once someone reports it — never to argue that it isn't there, and never as evidence a feel is solved. And when a build is named as good, the fastest honest path is to restore it, not to reconstruct what made it good.

Optional trait: drag-to-close

Sheets whose exit needs no confirmation may close by pulling down — an opt-in trait, granted per screen. Values from the meal-details reference:

tracks only when the sheet's scroller is at the top (scrollTop ≤ 0) · the sheet follows the finger with rubber-band resistance ×0.42 · release past 195px closes; short of it, the sheet springs back · during tracking the transition is off (.drag), so the sheet is glued to the finger · one scroller chains content scroll, header collapse and the dismiss pull in a single gesture — no separate grab zones.

Granted: meal details (reference), sign-in · withheld where closing loses work or aborts a journey mid-step (auth's OTP screen, auto-closing success) — those keep the X as the only exit, optionally with a confirm alert. The trait is per-screen, not per-flow: one flow can grant it on some screens and withhold it on others, decided by what an accidental pull would cost.
