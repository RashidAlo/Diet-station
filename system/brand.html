Brand rules

The few things that are law. Everything else in the system bends to taste — these don't.

The two reds

DietStation has two reds and they never trade places. The animation pink is the living ball in brand motion. The UI red is interface voice — buttons, accents, active states. Swapping them is the fastest way to make either feel off-brand.

White icons

Inside brand motion, the ball morphs into icons and glyphs — and those are always pure white. The pink ball and white icons are the entire cast; no third color ever joins the animation.

The laws

1
Never recolor the animation
Ball #FF3848, icons white. Not tints, not themes, not dark-mode variants. The choreography can change; the colors cannot.
2
One loud color per screen
UI red #ED1C24 is the only saturated voice in any view — one primary action, plus small accents (active states, kickers). If two things shout, neither does.
3
The canvas is Apple-light
Ground #F5F5F7, white cards, ink text. The brand lives in moments of red on calm gray — never a red or dark interface.
4
The wordmark is unicase Dot
Brand display moments set "diet station" in DietStation Dot — caps and lowercase share one form, so casing is a non-question. UI chrome uses Urbane ("DietStation" camel-cased).
5
Icons on red are white
Any glyph sitting on brand red or pink — buttons, badges, splash — renders white. On glass, adaptive contrast decides (#0B0E12 over light, white over dark).
DoRed CTA on a white card on the gray canvas; pink ball breathing on white or red splash; white dot-icons in motion.
Don'tPink buttons, red body text, tinted animation frames, a second saturated accent, or the ball in UI red.

Source of truth for motion is the Rive/Lottie pipeline — colors are baked there and verified on every export. See Foundations for the full palette and Typography for the Dot family.
